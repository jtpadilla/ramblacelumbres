This order brings together grasshoppers, crickets, locusts and the mole cricket; the last is an underground insect that feeds on the roots and necks of plants.

Most have vegetarian habits, though there are also omnivorous species. They are found in most land habitats. They are insects with incomplete metamorphosis: they go through three stages, egg, nymph and adult.

![Oedipoda caerulescens](../../../assets/uploads/2014/09/Oedipoda-caerulescens.jpg "*Oedipoda caerulescens*.")

## Grasshoppers

Grasshoppers and crickets are very much alike, although crickets have a stockier body. They are insects with great jumping power: they cover distances far greater than their body length. Medium to large in size, they have a big head, compound eyes and chewing mouthparts; they generally have two pairs of wings, fore and hind. Their hearing organs are on the abdomen. The sound never comes from the mouth: they produce it with stridulatory organs. Stridulation is the production of sound, the chirp made by rubbing the legs against the wings. They are usually active by day, and their antennae are short.

![Euchorthippus declivus](../../../assets/uploads/2014/09/Euchorthippus-declivus-2.jpg "*Euchorthippus declivus*.")
![Phaneroptera](../../../assets/uploads/2014/09/Phaneroptera.jpg "*Phaneroptera*, southern sickle-bearing bush-cricket.")

These insects may be solitary or gather in larger or smaller groups. In the most extreme cases they are gregarious, like the locusts —the name given to several species of grasshopper—, and sometimes become a plague on crops.

They need a few hours of morning sun to become active. They are harmless to people, unlike centipedes, certain spiders or wasps.

## Crickets

![Oecanthus pellucens](../../../assets/uploads/2014/09/Oecanthus-pellucens-1.jpg "*Oecanthus pellucens*.")

Most cricket species are active at dusk and at night, and have long antennae that sometimes exceed the length of the body. Their hearing organs are on the legs, unlike grasshoppers. The cricket produces its sound by rubbing its forewings together; the insistent “cri, cri” is a fundamental part of reproduction, among other things for attracting the female.

## The bush-cricket

*Steropleurus perezi*. Some bush-cricket species are herbivorous, while others are predatory and feed on other insects. They are very territorial.
