Dry-stone walls and shelters also add to the biodiversity of the area.

![Dry-stone hut](../../../assets/uploads/2014/08/39.jpg)

These constructions were needed to enlarge the area of cultivated fields. Dry-stone walls, called *marges*, form a wall that holds back the earth; in this way the fields absorb the rainwater and the seeds bear fruit.

![Dry-stone wall holding up a terrace](../../../assets/uploads/2014/08/25.jpg)
![Dry-stone wall](../../../assets/uploads/2014/08/16.jpg)

At other times the walls mark the boundary between paths and farms, and protect the crops from livestock.

The people of this land, with simple ideas, relying on logic and observation, with rudimentary tools and the technique learned from their ancestors, have raised true works of architecture with the only raw material taken from the land itself: the humble stone.

![Dry-stone hut among fields](../../../assets/uploads/2014/08/71.jpg)

The different kinds of hut depend on the needs of each place. Some, smaller, serve as shelter from bad weather, such as storms and downpours; others, larger, are used both as shelter and as a place to keep farm tools.

![Dry-stone shelter](../../../assets/uploads/2014/08/11.jpg)
![Inside a hut](../../../assets/uploads/2014/08/23.jpg)
![Dry-stone hut](../../../assets/uploads/2014/08/20.jpg)

They are constructions that blend into the landscape, lending naturalness and service to the place where they stand.

![Hut and dry-stone walls in the landscape](../../../assets/uploads/2014/08/MG_8784-2.jpg)
