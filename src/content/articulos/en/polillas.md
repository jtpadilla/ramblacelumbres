Along the banks and fallow fields, with the last rains, the grass has grown tall; you have to walk carefully, because everything is full of plants, as well as pine and juniper seedlings. In the morning you can still get a photograph or two of the butterflies, but once the sun starts to bite it is very difficult.

Moths have antennae of various kinds, but never clubbed like those of day-flying butterflies. Night-flying moths, besides their habits, have a thicker body and a hook-shaped projection on the inner edge of the hindwings.

![Moth among the vegetation](../../../assets/uploads/2014/04/12.jpg)

## Graellsia isabellae

When I saw this moth it was seven in the morning, at the dels Bassis spring (Cinctorres). It had just stopped raining and it blended in with the plants of its own colour. My brother picked it up and I was able to take several photographs on his hand, where you can see its size. Little by little, with the warmth it received through his skin, it began to move and, shortly after the sun reached it, to fly. It is of a surprising beauty and perfection. The specimen in the photograph is a male; the females have a rounded tail.

![Graellsia isabellae on a hand](../../../assets/uploads/2014/04/pasqua-2014-047.jpg "*Graellsia isabellae*, almost as big as a hand.")

## Adscita statices

Also known as the forester. This showy blue moth is found only in dry grassland, in July and August.

![Adscita statices](../../../assets/uploads/2014/04/Adscita-SP-F.ZYGAINADE-2.jpg "*Adscita statices*.")

## Capperia britanniodactylus

This interesting and little-known moth was hard for me to identify; we had not seen it around here before. At first I thought it was the better-known white plume moth, but they have nothing in common, as the colour is different.

![Capperia britanniodactylus](../../../assets/uploads/2014/04/Pterophorus-pentadactyla-penacho-de-cinco-plumas.jpg "*Capperia britanniodactylus*.")

## Eurranthis plummistaria

It can be seen in kermes-oak scrub and open pine woods. Its forewings are large in relation to its body, and its colours camouflage it very well. We have managed only one picture, as until now it was unknown to us.

![Eurranthis plummistaria](../../../assets/uploads/2014/04/Eurranthis-plummistaria.jpg "*Eurranthis plummistaria*.")
