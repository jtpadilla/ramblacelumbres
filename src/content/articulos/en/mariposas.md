Butterflies: *papallones*, *palometes*. We know butterflies as insects with wings covered in scales; I like to call them *palometes*, as I did when I was a girl.

Day-flying butterflies, when they rest, usually hold their wings together so that they look like a kind of sail above their body. They also differ from moths in that their antennae end in a club or knob.

![Scarce swallowtail](../../../assets/uploads/2014/06/164-Iphiclides-poladirius.jpg "*Iphiclides podalirius*.")

Of the various butterfly families of the Rambla Celumbres —Hesperiidae, Lycaenidae, Papilionidae, Pieridae and Nymphalidae—, the most numerous are those of the family Nymphalidae, and the least, those of the family Hesperiidae.
