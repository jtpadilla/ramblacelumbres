Among insects, ladybirds or coccinellids enjoy people's affection in every country: they feed on such harmful insects as aphids and, in general, are very beneficial to agriculture. The many popular names for these insects show how well known they are.

![Ladybird](../../../assets/uploads/2014/06/Coccinella-septempunctata-m.jpg "*Coccinella septempunctata*, ladybird.")

Their name goes back to the Middle Ages, when they were placed under the protection of the Virgin Mary and called “beetles of Our Lady”; in Italy they are known as “Our Lady's little hens”, in Germany as “God's little sheep”, in England as “Our Lady's birds” and in Spain as *mariquitas*. All the adults lie dormant through the winter: they can be found buried under dry scrub or in the cracks of holm oaks, piled one on top of another in great numbers in their winter shelters.

## Myrrha octodecimguttata

![Myrrha octodecimguttata](../../../assets/uploads/2014/06/Mirra-octodecimguttata1.jpg "*Myrrha octodecimguttata*.")

A beetle of the coccinellid family, like the ladybirds. In this part of the Els Ports region it is very rarely seen; in fact, this was the first time I had seen and photographed it.

## Lachnaia tristigma

Commonly known as the six-spot beetle. It belongs to the family Chrysomelidae; it is not like the ladybirds, although its colours make it look so.

## Exosoma lusitanicum

The last day we went out into the countryside, many yellow flowers had a specimen sipping nectar, its coppery colour standing out strongly against them.

## Acmaeodera degener

Also called the fourteen-spot beetle, it is very showy and little appreciated, as it feeds on the wood of oaks and holm oaks. It stands out strongly among flowers of various colours.
