Diurnal birds of prey are predators. They live in the highest places of the mountain ranges of this area. They are hunters, and have developed such features as excellent eyesight for spotting their prey, far more perfect than ours. To seize and tear their prey they are equipped with talons and very sharp beaks.

They vary in outward form according to their way of life and their diet, which is diverse.

## Griffon vulture

![Griffon vulture](../../../assets/uploads/2014/08/3.jpg)

On the limestone cliffs of the Roca Parda, in the Rambla Celumbres, scavenging raptors are common; they feed on carrion and so play a regulating role in the ecosystems where they live. Such is the griffon vulture (*Gyps fulvus*), which is grouped in the order Falconiformes, lives in colonies and is one of the largest birds of prey to be found in the Iberian Peninsula.

To get these photographs, my brother Tadeo and I, Paquita, spent several mornings crouched under a camouflage net at the very top of the rocky cliff, until we managed to capture their majestic flight.

![Gyps fulvus, griffon vulture](../../../assets/uploads/2014/08/10.jpg "*Gyps fulvus*, griffon vulture, *voltor*.")
![Griffon vulture soaring](../../../assets/uploads/2014/08/5-2.jpg)

The griffon vulture spends much of its time gliding effortlessly, without beating its wings, rising in search of the thermals on which it travels. Its wings form a right angle with its body.

![Griffon vulture](../../../assets/uploads/2014/08/Gyps-fulvus-buitre-14.jpg)

It is a very large bird, with a wingspan of more than 2.50 metres. Its head is almost bald, with short down, and it has a ruff of feathers around the neck. The colour of its plumage is like a lion's, which is probably where its Spanish name, *buitre leonado*, comes from. It is sedentary and gregarious.

![Griffon vultures on the cliff](../../../assets/uploads/2014/08/7.jpg)

## Peregrine falcon

Falcons have an elegant, proud look. Their wings are slender and their body sleek; they can dive at great speed on their prey, generally birds.

![Peregrine falcon](../../../assets/uploads/2014/08/Falco-peregrinus-alcon-2.jpg "*Falco peregrinus*, peregrine falcon.")

In these dives, peregrine falcons reach speeds of more than 300 kilometres an hour. They lay their eggs in a small hollow or on a sheltered rock ledge, and usually change nest every few years. Some pairs use the empty nests of other birds to lay their eggs, between March and April. It is very aggressive towards other birds of prey.
