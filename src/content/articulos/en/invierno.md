In winter, whether a tree is deciduous or evergreen is the result of its adaptation to its surroundings and to the climate. Evergreens need their leaves to protect themselves from the cold; deciduous trees, on the other hand, stop absorbing nutrients from the soil, enter their winter rest and let their leaves fall. They stand bare.

When the snow comes the aquifers are replenished, the earth absorbs the nutrients from the dry leaves and seems to doze, waiting for fine weather.
