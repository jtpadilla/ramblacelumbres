![Ripe corn](../../../assets/uploads/2015/07/MG_9615.jpg "Corn ripe for harvest.")

Summer is the season of the year that, astronomically, begins at the summer solstice —this year it fell on 21 June 2015— and ends at the autumn equinox.

For many cultures it was not merely an excuse to celebrate or pray: it was something essential to their existence. Linked to agriculture, the summer solstice was a reminder that the crops had reached their peak.

This region has been, and still is, mainly cereal-growing. With the spring rains, the cereal harvest comes more or less early. The way the harvest is gathered now has nothing to do with how it was done not so many years ago, when the manual work was very hard and the season began in June or July.

Although cereals —wheat, barley and rye— are still important and necessary, they are now grown only in large fields with easy access for the combine harvesters.

Various aromatic plants are also harvested, from which come the essential oils sold by a cooperative, helping the economy of this land, harsh because of its climate and frequent drought, except for the summer storms, which sometimes cause damage.
